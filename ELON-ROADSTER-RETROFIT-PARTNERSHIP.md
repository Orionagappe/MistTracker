**Challenge 1: Distance/Communication Lag**
- Current distance: 200+ million km
- Signal delay: ~15 minutes one-way
- Solution: Autonomous mode (onboard processing), batch data downloads

**Challenge 2: Radiation Environment**
- Heliocentric orbit exposes equipment to solar radiation + cosmic rays
- Existing solar panels degraded by 8 years of exposure
- Solution: Radiation-hardened electronics, shielded compartments, redundancy

**Challenge 3: Structural Integrity**
- Roadster untouched for 8 years in vacuum + thermal cycling
- Unknown condition of seals, electronics, structural integrity
- Solution: Pre-retrofit inspection (rendezvous mission needed)

**Challenge 4: Power System**
- Original solar panels degraded
- Battery unknown condition
- Solution: Complete power system replacement

**Challenge 5: Launch/Deployment**
- Can't modify Roadster in situ; needs retrieval or in-situ upgrade
- Options:
  - **Option A**: Rendezvous mission to physically retrofit (expensive, complex)
  - **Option B**: Autonomous arrival of instrument package (detaches, docks)
  - **Option C**: Staged deployment (Phase 1: Deploy basic sensors, Phase 2: Add advanced)

## SECTION 4: LEGAL & REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

### 4.1 Outer Space Treaty Compliance

**UN Outer Space Treaty (1967)** - Key Provisions:

| Provision | Implication for Roadster Retrofit | Mitigation |
|-----------|---|---|
| **Article II**: No national appropriation | US retains registration; no sovereignty claim | Comply with intent; treat as international asset |
| **Article VIII**: Registration of space objects | Must update FAA registration with new mission | Submit updated Form 7915.1 to FAA |
| **Article VI**: National responsibility for non-governmental activities | US responsible for Orion's compliance | Orion operates under FAA licensed missions |
| **Article IX**: Avoid contamination | Ensure no biological/chemical contamination | Standard decontamination procedures |
| **Article VII**: Liability for damage | Orion liable for damage caused by Roadster | Obtain appropriate insurance ($500M+ coverage typical) |

**Compliance Path**:
1. Update FAA registration (new mission description, operators, objectives)
2. File notification with UN (standard procedure for mission changes)
3. Obtain FAA launch license if deploying new payload module
4. Insurance: Obtain space liability insurance (third-party damage coverage)
5. Deorbit planning: Plan eventual disposal (per Article IX)

### 4.2 US Commercial Space Licensing

**Required Licenses/Permits**:
1. **Experimental Spacecraft License** - If modifying Roadster structure/operations
2. **Launch License** - If deploying any satellite/module to meet it

4. **Frequency Coordination** - With FCC for X-band radio operations

**Timeline**: 12-18 months for all permits (standard for innovative missions)
