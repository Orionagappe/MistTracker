# Type 4 Authorization: Corporate Participants

**Date:** May 5, 2026  
**Classification:** Type 4 (Data Subscribers)  
**Status:** Framework Defined  
**Framework:** SLM with Type OMEGA

---

## Authorization Statement

**Type 4** is Corporate. They pay a fee.

They get numbers.

Nothing else.

They make money off validator dreams. That's the deal. They observe, analyze, monetize insights from validator behavior.

They don't control validators. They don't own validators. They don't extract value from validator earnings.

**They buy metrics. That's it.**

---

## Core Principle

**Type 4 is pure data play.**

Corporations pay for access to validator performance metrics. They can:
- Analyze which strategies work
- Identify emerging talent
- Predict validator success patterns
- Build training programs based on observed data
- Sell insights and analysis to validators/teams

What they cannot do:
- Access validator accounts or private data
- Take revenue cut from validator earnings
- Sponsor validators to guarantee favorable outcomes
- Manipulate metrics or hide information
- Influence Byzantine consensus decisions

**The relationship is observational, not extractive.**

---

## Scope of Type 4 Access

**What They Pay For:**
- Real-time validator reputation metrics
- Sandbox performance statistics
- Byzantine consensus voting patterns
- Universe influence tracking (which validators affect what)
- Performance trending and prediction models
- Leaderboard data and rankings
- Match outcome data and AI decision logs

**Access Level:**
- Public data (everything validators can see about themselves and leaderboards)
- Aggregated statistics (trends across validator population, no individual privacy violation)
- Historical data (all past performance, all past sandbox results)
- Prediction APIs (plug in validator data, get predicted performance model)

**Format:**
- Raw JSON data feeds (for data scientists)
- Dashboard analytics (for business analysts)
- Custom report generation (for management)
- API access (for third-party platforms)

**Restrictions:**
- Cannot see validator private correspondence
- Cannot see validator reasoning/journals/strategy documents
- Cannot see classified materials (Type 0, Type 1, Type OMEGA docs)
- Cannot manipulate or alter data
- Cannot hide data from public (audit chain shows everything)

## How Type 4 Works

### The Business Model

**Corporations pay subscription fee:**
- Tier 1: $50K/month — Basic metrics + aggregated statistics
- Tier 2: $200K/month — Full API access + real-time data feeds
- Tier 3: $500K/month — Predictive models + custom analytics

**They get:**
- Data exports (CSV, JSON, database dumps)
- Live metric feeds (WebSocket real-time updates)
- Reporting tools (custom dashboards)
- API access (integrate validator data into their platforms)

**They make money by:**
- Selling analysis to validators ($1000-$10K per custom report)
- Running training programs (coaching based on data insights)
- Sponsoring validator teams (branded teams with analytics support)
- Selling to streamers/media (highlight reels, predicted upset matches)
- Licensing prediction models to other esports platforms

### The Ethical Boundary

**What's prohibited:**

1. **Revenue extraction** — Can't take cuts from validator earnings
2. **Control** — Can't influence which civilization wins or how votes go
3. **Secrecy** — Can't hide data or use it for insider trading on ladder matches
4. **Monopoly** — Can't be the only data provider; data must be available to all corporate subscribers
5. **Manipulation** — Can't alter metrics or hide information from public view

**What's permitted:**

1. **Analysis** — Understand validator behavior from data
2. **Prediction** — Build models that forecast validator success
3. **Coaching** — Teach validators what the data says works
4. **Monetization** — Sell analysis and coaching to validators
5. **Sponsorship** — Fund validators in exchange for branding

- No commercial ecosystem
- Validators have no sponsorship/coaching/funding sources
- Esports platform operates in vacuum
- No incentive for professional development
- No secondary market for insights

**With Type 4:**
- Professional esports ecosystem emerges
- Validators can get paid to play (via sponsorships)

- Commercial incentives align with system health

**The key constraint:** They get data, not control. They profit from observation, not manipulation.

If a corporation tries to hide data or manipulate validators, the audit chain exposes it. All their transactions are logged. All their API calls are auditable.

**Type 4 operates in the light.**

## Data Rights & Privacy

### What's Public

All metrics are visible to everyone (validators, corporations, auditors):
- Individual validator reputation scores
- Sandbox performance statistics
- Byzantine quorum voting records (anonymized voting patterns, not voter identity if privacy requested)
- Universe influence logs (which proposals affect universe and by how much)
- Leaderboards and rankings

### What's Private (No Sale)

- Validator personal correspondence
- Private strategy documents/journals
- Account security information
- Financial details (how much validators earned, if they sold data rights)
- Validator identity (handles only, can remain pseudonymous)

### Data Rights

Corporations can sell analysis and insights, but:
- Cannot sell raw validator data itself (no "download this validator's full history and resell it")
- Cannot claim they own the data (audit chain is source of truth)
- Cannot hide that data came from Stellaris validators
- Must give credit: "Based on SLM framework validator data"

---

## Type 4 Ethics

The system respects Type 4 because they're honest about what they do:

**"We profit by observing and analyzing validator behavior."**

Not: "We partner with validators to maximize their earnings" (revenue extraction)  
Not: "We provide coaching programs" (if they're not data-based)  
Not: "We ensure fair competition" (they don't control fairness)  

**Honesty = legitimacy.**

If Type 4 corporations try to pretend they're not extractive when they are, they lose subscription rights. The audit chain catches hidden arrangements.

But if they say "We buy data, sell analysis, validators choose whether to work with us," that's transparent and valid.

## Conclusion

**Type 4 is "pay for numbers and monetize what you learn."**

The system:
- Wants commercial ecosystem (validators need funding sources)
- Trusts data (audit chain is transparent)
- Doesn't trust control (corporates can't manipulate validators)
- Rewards honesty (hidden arrangements trigger security review)

Corporations succeed by making validators successful. The incentives align: better validators = more interesting data = more valuable insights.

They make money off validator dreams, but validators keep their own autonomy.

That's the deal.
