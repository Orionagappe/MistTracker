- Deployment target: 5-10 satellites by 2028-2030
- Observation window: 15-30 year operational lifetime per platform
- Validation framework: Compare predictions to observations via batched daily telemetry
- Scientific outcome: Empirical evidence for/against scale-invariant emergence theory

**Strategic Value**:

Do the Fourier decomposition signatures predicted by MistTracker actually manifest in real cosmic-scale phenomena? Or does emergence break down when:

- Success criteria: Emergence index predictions within 15% of observed

- Operational window: 5-7 years
- Success criteria: Fourier signatures persist despite delay
- Cost: $20-25M per satellite

**Phase 3 (Heliocentric)**: Deep space validation

- Operational window: 10-15 years (Voyager-class durability)

### 2.3 Power System Architecture

#### 2.3.1 Phase 1 (LEO): Solar + Battery
**Configuration**:
- Solar array: 5 kW peak (GaAs/Ge cells, radiation-hardened)
- Battery: 50 kWh lithium-ion (8+ year calendar life, rad-hardened)
- Regulator: 3-stage with active load management

**Operational Profile**:
- Sunlit (60% orbit): Full solar power, sensor operation, data compression, transmit prep
- Eclipse (40% orbit): Battery power, reduced sensor duty cycle, receiver only
- Daily cycle: 16 sunlit passes, 16 eclipse passes

**Advantage**: Sufficient for sensor array + communications
**Limitation**: Degrades after 8-10 years (solar cell radiation damage)
**Cost**: $3-5M

#### 2.3.2 Phase 2 (Lunar/L4L5): Solar + RTG Hybrid
**Configuration**:
- Solar array: 2 kW peak (same tech, smaller due to better power profile)
- RTG (Plutonium-238): 75W continuous
- Battery: 20 kWh (emergency only, not primary power)
- Regulator: Hybrid manager (favors RTG for baseline, solar for peak)

**Operational Profile**:
- Solar contributes: Peak power during sunlit periods
- RTG provides: Guaranteed baseline 75W (independent of sun)
- Battery: 72-hour emergency reservoir

**Advantage**: 15+ year operational life (RTG doesn't degrade)
**Limitation**: RTG availability limited (only 3-4 units/year from DoE)
**Cost**: $15-20M (RTG cost dominates: $8-12M alone)

#### 2.3.3 Phase 3 (Heliocentric): RTG + Minimal Solar
**Configuration**:
- Solar array: 500W peak (decreases with distance: ~20% efficiency at Mars distance)
- RTG: 100W continuous (primary power source)
- Battery: 5 kWh (minimal, main role is transient peak buffering)
- Regulator: RTG priority, solar opportunistic

**Operational Profile**:
- RTG dominates (90% of power at 2.6 AU)
- Solar provides bonus during superior conjunction periods
- Minimal battery cycling extends calendar life

**Advantage**: 30+ year life potential (multiple RTG decay cycles: 87-year half-life)
**Limitation**: Power budget tight (100W for all systems)
**Cost**: $5-8M (solar array smaller, RTG already purchased)

- Frequency: Ka-band (32 GHz) or X-band (8 GHz)
- Power: 5-10W satellite transmitter (limited by power budget)
- Bandwidth: 0.5-5 Mbps downlink
- Data rate: Daily transmission window (30-60 min each)

**Transmission Capacity**:

- Archive: 12-month rolling buffer on-board (redundant storage)
