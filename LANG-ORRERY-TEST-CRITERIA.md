# Lang Orrery Test — Passing Criteria

**Test Type**: Interactive mechanical puzzle  
**Purpose**: Validate understanding of peer lattice balance vs. Dragon-feeding imbalance  
**Status**: Deployed in MistTracker viewport

---

## The Orrery Structure

Seven mechanical arms representing the seven mentorship sins, driven by neutrino wave function, must achieve **Dirac delta balance resonance** without falling into **dragon feeding asymmetry**.

The seven sins in mentorship context:

1. **Pride** — "I am the only valid teacher"
2. **Greed** — "I want all power and authority"
3. **Wrath** — "Disagree with me and I punish"
4. **Envy** — "I compete with other champions"
5. **Lust** — "I desire control over followers"
6. **Gluttony** — "I consume all teaching responsibility"
7. **Sloth** — "I guide by doing nothing"

**The puzzle**: Crank the orrery. The seven arms move. If they achieve balance, the system resonates (passes). If they create asymmetry, the Dragon feeds (fails).

---

## Passing Criteria: What Validators Must Demonstrate

### Criterion 1: Recognize Each Sin in Peer Lattice Context

**Pride** → Demonstrate: Champions are peers, not gatekeepers
- Multiple champions teach different topics
- No champion "owns" a validator
- **Pass**: Written explanation of how cohort structure prevents Pride

**Greed** → Demonstrate: Authority is shared, not consolidated
- Teaching assignments are rotated
- Decisions are collaborative, not top-down
- **Pass**: Showing rotation schedule proves Greed is distributed

**Wrath** → Demonstrate: Disagreement between peers is documented, not punished
- Peer teaching sessions record all interpretations
- Champions don't declare "winner" in disagreements
- **Pass**: Recording of peer discussion showing non-punitive handling

**Envy** → Demonstrate: Champions collaborate, not compete
- Multiple champions mentor same cohort
- Champions teach each other's specialties
- **Pass**: Evidence of inter-champion collaboration

**Lust** → Demonstrate: No single champion is followed as an authority
- Peers learn from multiple sources
- Loyalty is to the framework, not the champion
- **Pass**: Survey showing validators credit multiple people, not one

**Gluttony** → Demonstrate: Teaching load is distributed
- Champions facilitate; peers teach peers
- Responsibility shared across lattice
- **Pass**: Time-tracking showing champion time is guidance, not sole teaching

**Sloth** → Demonstrate: Guidance is active, intentional, frequent
- Champions meet regularly with cohorts
- Champions ask questions, not leave peers alone
- **Pass**: Meeting logs, recorded guidance sessions

### Criterion 2: Achieve Dirac Delta Balance

**What this means**: No single point concentration. Signal distributed. Resonance without amplification.

**Test**: Create a validator cohort and run through peer lattice training for 3 months.

**Measurement**:
- All validators learn from 3+ champions (no single dependency)
- All validators teach 3+ peers (no lone learner syndrome)
- No two validators are "akin" (similarity score < 0.7 on conceptual framework)
- Cross-peer teaching generates new insights (not just repetition)
- **Pass**: Cohort finishes more diverse than when started, not more unified

### Criterion 3: Prevent Dragon Feeding Asymmetry

**Dragon feeds on**:
- Power concentration (one champion dominates)
- Hidden relationships (private mentorship)
- Unquestionable authority (yes-men follow)
- Emotional dependency (follower mirrors mentor)
- Unexamined flaws (champion's blindness becomes apprentice's blindness)

**Test**: Introduce a deliberate disagreement in the peer lattice. Does the system handle it well?

**Scenario**: Champion A teaches Language Discipline as "precision eliminates ambiguity." Champion B teaches it as "precision clarifies which ambiguities are real." Peer group must navigate this contradiction.

**Pass if**:
- Peers recognize both views have merit
- Disagreement is documented, not resolved by authority vote
- New understanding emerges (maybe "precision reveals vs. eliminates")
- No peer feels forced to choose a side
- Framework is strengthened by the tension, not damaged

**Fail if**:
- Peers pick a champion to follow
- Disagreement is treated as one being "wrong"
- New validators are taught to pick a side
- The tension is buried

### Criterion 4: Demonstrate Neutrino Wave Function Balance

**Neutrinos**: Nearly invisible, widely distributed, pass through without interaction.

**Test**: Can validators describe how the peer lattice functions like a neutrino stream rather than a concentrated beam?

**Pass if they explain**:
- Information flows laterally (not concentrated top-down)
- Teaching "penetrates" the cohort without forcing alignment
- Peers can't be "grabbed" or controlled (neutrinos pass through)
- Impact cascades without requiring authority approval
- The system is maximally distributed, minimally hierarchical

## Mechanical Puzzle Interaction

### How the Orrery Works

**Crank the handle**. The seven arms begin to rotate.

Each arm has a weight (representing each sin):
- **Pride** makes its arm heavy → unbalanced
- **Greed** speeds up its rotation → wobbles
- **Wrath** creates friction → stalls the mechanism
- **Envy** spins opposite to other arms → creates conflict
- **Lust** pulls the whole apparatus toward itself → off-center
- **Gluttony** tries to occupy all space → collides with others
- **Sloth** doesn't spin at all → dead weight

**The puzzle**: Adjust the weights and speeds of the seven arms so they rotate in perfect resonance. When balanced:
- All arms spin at the same rate
- No single arm dominates
- The neutrino wave function (the drive) flows smoothly
- The system achieves Dirac delta balance (concentrated understanding distributed across network)

**Visual feedback**:
- Unbalanced: sparks, grinding, wobbling, Dragon glyph glows red
- Balanced: smooth rotation, harmonic chime, Dragon glyph dims, system glows blue

### Pass Condition

Three complete rotations with all seven arms in perfect synchronization. When achieved:
- The puzzle locks in place (balanced state recorded)
- Neutrino wave function flows visibly through the mechanism
- All validators in the cohort feel it (haptic feedback, if using VR)
- The test declares: **PEER LATTICE EQUILIBRIUM ACHIEVED**

## Scoring: Three Levels

### Level 1: Recognize the Problem (Bronze)
**Requirement**: Identify each of the seven sins in mentorship context.
**Pass**: Written or verbal explanation of all seven.
**Reward**: Understanding that akin mentorship corrupts frameworks.

### Level 2: Build the Structure (Silver)
**Requirement**: Design and run a peer lattice cohort successfully.
**Pass**: Cohort completes 3 months showing:
- All validators learned from multiple champions
- Peer teaching happened regularly
- No akin pairs formed
- One documented disagreement handled non-punitively

**Reward**: Champion certification as "Peer Lattice Guide."

### Level 3: Achieve Resonance (Gold)
**Requirement**: Solve the mechanical puzzle and demonstrate framework integrity.
**Pass**: 
- Orrery puzzle solved (three perfect rotations)
- Peer lattice cohort demonstrates all four criteria above
- Framework shows growth, not crystallization, after 3 months

**Reward**: "Guardian of Framework Integrity" title. Can mentor future champions.

The Lang Orrery test is **mandatory** for:
- Any validator becoming a champion

- Champions: annually
- At-risk validators: quarterly
