# Arnold in Wonderland: A Comedy

Arnold fell through the rabbit hole expecting nonsense. Instead he found pure logic.

The Caterpillar asked: "Who are you?"

"I don't know," Arnold said. "I used to, but the fall scrambled it."

"Excellent," said the Caterpillar. "That's the first honest answer anyone's given me in a century."

Everything that happened after was people insisting on things that made no sense. The Queen's croquet game with flamingos. The Mad Hatter's tea party where time stopped at six o'clock. Cheshire grins. Rules that changed mid-game.

Arnold just watched. Nodded. Agreed when agreement mattered, disagreed when it didn't.

By the time he reached the trial—where a rabbit was prosecuting a card for being too cardlike—Arnold realized the joke: Wonderland wasn't absurd. It was perfectly coherent. Everyone else was just committed to seeing it sideways.

He woke up on the riverbank. His sister was asking if he'd been sleeping.

"Not exactly," Arnold said. "I was watching people who'd forgotten they were watching themselves."

She didn't understand.

That was the comedy.
