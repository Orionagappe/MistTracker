### Day 3-4: Radio Astronomy Fundamentals

**Lecture 1.2: How Radio Telescopes Work**

*The Big Ear* (1973-1998):
- Fixed parabolic reflector: 110m × 74m
- Feed horn: Receives at 1420 MHz (hydrogen line)
- Receiver chain: Amplifier → Mixer → Intermediate frequency → Detector
- Output: Chart recorder (ink on paper—primitive but honest)
- Sensitivity: ~1 jansky (very weak signals)

*Signal Path*:
1. Cosmic radio source → reflected by parabolic dish
2. Focused on feed horn → electrical signal (nanovolts)
3. Amplified 10^6× → matched to reference frequency
4. Mixed → lower frequency (~10 kHz) → easier to measure
5. Detected → paper output (deflection = signal strength)
6. Recorded → permanent record (cannot erase, cannot manipulate)

*Why This Detection Method Matters*:
- Single beam (cannot pinpoint source exactly)
- No digital data (impossible to reprocess later)
- Chart recorder (raw mechanical output, visible bias immediately)
- No frequency resolution (doesn't distinguish narrow signal from noise)

**Key Concept: 1420 MHz**

Why this frequency?
- Hydrogen atom's natural emission line (hyperfine transition)
- Cold hydrogen throughout universe emits here
- Choice frequency for SETI search
- Natural cosmic background at this frequency

**Lecture 1.3: Cosmic Radio Background**

*What's in Space at 1420 MHz*:
- Galactic plane: ~1000 K equivalent background (strongest)
- Galactic poles: ~10 K background (coldest)
- Extragalactic sources: Distant galaxies, pulsars, quasars
- Cosmic microwave background: 2.7 K (weakest, most uniform)

*Signal-to-Noise Challenge*:
- Wow! signal: 30× background noise = 30:1 signal-to-noise ratio
- Significance: ~5σ detection (very strong, clear signal)
- Problem: One detection doesn't prove origin

### Day 3-4: Inference Boundaries

**Lecture 2.2: The Inference Cliff**

*Observation*: Signal detected at 30× background level, 1420 MHz

*Inference Chain* (each step adds assumption):

**Step 1: Location**
- Observation: Signal detected during scan of Chi Sagittarii region
- Inference: Signal originated in that region (vs. sidelobe contamination, cosmic scatter)
- Assumption: Antenna beam was pointing correctly (untested for this signal)

**Step 2: Frequency**
- Observation: Receiver tuned to 1420.4055 MHz detected signal
- Inference: Signal is narrowband at that frequency (vs. broadband, reflected from satellite)
- Assumption: Receiver frequency calibration is accurate (not validated per-signal)

**Step 3: Polarization**
- Observation: Signal detected (Big Ear measured intensity only)
- Inference: Polarization unknown
- BUT often inferred: Signal is unpolarized (wrong inference—unpolarized means we don't know)
- Risk: Treating "unknown" as "natural emission" (natural emissions are polarized!)

**Step 4: Origin**
- Observation: Signal detected at 1420 MHz (hydrogen line)
- Inference: Signal is extraterrestrial (vs. ground-based, satellite, atmospheric)
- Assumption: All earthly sources excluded (only assumed, not proven)
- Risk: Confirmation bias (signals at hydrogen line → SETI interpretation)

**Step 5: Intelligence**
- Observation: Narrowband signal detected
- Inference: Signal is artificial (vs. natural celestial source)
- Assumption: Natural sources cannot produce narrowband signals (FALSE—pulsars do)
- Risk: Major error (false indicator of intelligence)

## WEEK 3: Measurement Critique—Systematic Uncertainty

### Day 1-2: Systematic Error Analysis

**Lecture 3.1: What Could Go Wrong**

*Systematic Errors* (constant bias, not random noise):

**Error 1: Antenna Sidelobe Contamination**
- Antenna has main beam + sidelobes (weak responses in other directions)
- Risk: Strong signal from nearby direction (not Chi Sagittarii) could appear as signal from target
- Big Ear issue: Fixed antenna, can't steer to confirm location
- Consequence: Wow! could be from completely different direction

**Error 2: Receiver Saturation**
- Receiver can be overloaded by very strong signal
- Overloaded receiver produces strange artifacts (may look like narrowband signal)
- Risk: Wow! could be saturation artifact, not real signal
- Check: Would require looking at receiver gain during signal

**Error 3: Frequency Drift**
- Receiver oscillator stability (1977 electronics not perfect)
- Risk: Receiver frequency calibration off by 1 MHz (would misidentify signal frequency)
- Consequence: Wow! might not be at 1420 MHz (weakens SETI interpretation)

**Error 4: Digital Artifacts**
- Big Ear had no digital recording (only chart paper output)
- Risk: Pen oscillation, chart feed irregularity could mimic signal pattern
- Consequence: What looks like signal might be mechanical artifact

**Error 5: Interference Contamination**
- Radio frequency interference (RFI) from ground-based transmitters
- 1977: Growing number of transmitters, Earth orbit filling with satellites
- Risk: Wow! could be interference, not cosmic signal
- Check: Did Ohio find any RFI sources active Aug 15, 1977?

**Lecture 3.2: Uncertainty Quantification**

*For Wow! Signal*:

$$\sigma_{amplitude} = \sqrt{\sigma_{calibration}^2 + \sigma_{noise}^2 + \sigma_{systematic}^2}$$

Where:
- $\sigma_{calibration}$ = Amplitude measurement uncertainty (±5%)
- $\sigma_{noise}$ = Statistical noise variation (±10%)
- $\sigma_{systematic}$ = Unknown systematic errors (±20%?)

**Result**: True signal amplitude could be 25× to 35× background (not exactly 30×)

*Significance Impact*:
- If actual amplitude 25×: Significance drops from 5σ to 4σ
- If actual amplitude 35×: Significance increases to 5.5σ
- Range: Still significant, but uncertainty matters

## WEEK 5: Statistical Analysis + Voyager Validation

### Day 1-2: Significance and False Positives

**Lecture 5.1: Why 3σ Isn't Magic**

*The Problem*: Wow! signal was 30× background (very significant). Yet 49 years later, no confirmation.

*Statistical Explanation*:

$$P(\text{signal exists}) \neq P(\text{observed 30x amplitude}) $$

- Observation: 30× amplitude signal → ~5σ detection
- Meaning: Probability of this observation IF background-only = 1 in 3.5 million
- BUT: Probability that real signal caused this observation ≠ 1 in 3.5 million

*Why?* Bayes' theorem:

$$P(\text{signal} \mid \text{observation}) = \frac{P(\text{observation} \mid \text{signal}) \times P(\text{signal})}{P(\text{observation})}$$

- $P(\text{observation} \mid \text{signal})$ = Given real signal, probability we see this ≈ 99%
- $P(\text{signal})$ = Prior probability extraterrestrial signal exists at this moment ≈ 10^-12
- $P(\text{observation})$ = Overall probability of seeing this amplitude ≈ 3×10^-7

Result:
$$P(\text{signal} \mid \text{observation}) \approx \frac{0.99 \times 10^{-12}}{3 \times 10^{-7}} \approx 10^{-6}$$

**Conclusion**: Despite 5σ observation, posterior probability signal is real ≈ 1 in a million (due to extremely low prior).

**Key Lesson**: Statistical significance ≠ Physical truth. Bayesian reasoning required.

**Lecture 5.2: Look-Elsewhere Effect**

*Problem*: If you search many potential sources, you'll find ~significant results by chance.

*Example*:
- Search 1000 frequency channels for signal
- Each channel has 5% false-positive rate (20σ event per 10^6 trials)
- Expected false positives: 1000 × 0.05 = 50 "significant" results

*Wow! Context*:
- Big Ear searched continuously (millions of source locations × frequencies)
- If searching this broadly, finding one 5σ event is expected by chance
- Conclusion: Wow! is not special statistically (expected rate of false alarms)

**Remedy**:
- Pre-register hypothesis (specify what you're looking for before looking)
- Correct for look-elsewhere (divide significance by number of searches)
- Use independent data set (test on different data than you trained on)

## WEEK 6: Enigma Investigation—Holding Paradox

**Lecture 6.1: The Wow! Paradox**

*What We Know*:
- Signal was detected (documented, real event)
- Signal was strong (30× background, ~5σ)
- Signal was unique (never repeated)
- Signal's origin is unknown (49 years, still unexplained)

*The Paradox*:
- Can be real AND unexplained (cosmic transient, rare one-time event)
- Can be artifact AND unexplained (unknown systematic from 1977)
- Can be intelligent AND one-time (signal sent, then transmitter inactive)
- Can be natural AND mysterious (new pulsar type we don't recognize yet)

*Validator Challenge*:
- Cannot collapse into single hypothesis without investigation
- Must hold all possibilities simultaneously
- Cannot claim comfort (admission of ignorance)
- Must remain open to evidence
