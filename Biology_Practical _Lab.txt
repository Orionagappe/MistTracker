Know the basic lab safety rules.

Always wear safety goggles, gloves, and lab coats. Know the location of safety equipment. Never eat, drink, or run in the lab.



Know why it is necessary for everyone to have a fundamental knowledge of biology.

Biology helps us understand living systems, health, and the environment. It informs medical, environmental, and ethical decisions. Basic knowledge is essential for informed citizenship.



Recognize the different types of variables that may be present in an experiment.

Independent variable (changed), dependent variable (measured), and controlled variables (kept constant).



Explain the advantage of using the metric system over the system we use in the United States.

The metric system is decimal-based, universal, and easier for scientific calculations and conversions.



Identify the basic units of measure in the metric system.

Meter (length), gram (mass), liter (volume), and second (time).



Know the pH of a neutral solution.

pH 7 is neutral.



Be able to calculate the difference in hydrogen ion concentration between two different solutions.

Each pH unit represents a tenfold difference in [H+]. For example, pH 4 is 100 times more acidic than pH 6.



Know what the positive reactions look like when testing for sugar, starch, or protein.

Benedict’s test (sugar): orange/red; Iodine (starch): blue-black; Biuret (protein): violet.



Be able to identify the reagents used to detect sugars, starch, lipids, and proteins.

Benedict’s (sugar), iodine (starch), Sudan III or brown paper (lipids), Biuret (protein).



Define magnification, resolution, and contrast.

Magnification: enlarging image; resolution: clarity/detail; contrast: difference between light/dark areas.




Explain the purpose of a wet mount.

Allows observation of living cells/tissues in a natural, hydrated state.



Identify the various parts of a microscope.

Parts: eyepiece, objective lenses, stage, coarse/fine focus, light source, diaphragm, arm, base.



State differences between eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells.

Eukaryotes have nuclei and organelles; prokaryotes lack both. Eukaryotes are usually larger.



Recognize an animal cell or a plant cell under a microscope and identify one cell structure that differs between these two cell types.

Plant cells have cell walls and chloroplasts; animal cells do not. Look for a large central vacuole in plants.



Know the source of bromelain in our experiment on enzyme activity.

Bromelain comes from pineapple.



Explain what is lost when a protein is denatured.

The protein loses its three-dimensional structure and function.



Identify factors that could influence the rate of diffusion.

Temperature, concentration gradient, molecule size, and medium (solid/liquid/gas).



Define osmosis, hypotonic, hypertonic, and isotonic.

Osmosis: water movement across a membrane. Hypotonic: lower solute; hypertonic: higher solute; isotonic: equal solute.



Describe the function of stomata.

Stomata allow gas exchange (CO2 in, O2 and water vapor out) in plant leaves.



Identify the process commonly used to separate leaf pigments.

Paper chromatography.



Identify the ultimate source of energy powering photosynthesis.

Sunlight.



Identify the reasons bromothymol blue turned yellow, then blue again, during the photosynthesis lab.

Yellow: CO2 produced (acidic); blue: CO2 used up during photosynthesis (solution becomes basic/neutral).


Identify the gas that caused the balloon to expand in the respiration lab and the reaction that produced it.

CO2 from cellular respiration (glucose breakdown).



Name the products of aerobic respiration.

CO2, H2O, and ATP.



Describe the function of ATP in a cell.

ATP stores and provides energy for cellular processes.



Identify the phase of mitosis shown by each of several plant cell cycle models.

Identify by chromosome arrangement: prophase (condensing), metaphase (aligned), anaphase (separating), telophase (two groups).



Know the types of cells that undergo mitosis and meiosis.

Mitosis: somatic (body) cells. Meiosis: germ cells (produce gametes).



Describe the end product, including the number of end product, of meiosis.

Four non-identical haploid cells (gametes).



Describe the difference between diploid and haploid cells.

Diploid: two sets of chromosomes (2n); haploid: one set (n).



Determine the genotype possibilities resulting from a cross between two heterozygous parents.

Genotypes: 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa (if A = dominant, a = recessive).



Be able to recognize the 9:3:3:1 phenotype ratio in the results of a dihybrid cross.

9:3:3:1 ratio = two traits, both parents heterozygous for both genes.



Determine the possible blood types of a child, given the blood type of one parent.

Depends on both parents’ genotypes; use Punnett square for combinations (A, B, AB, O).



Contrast replication with transcription.

Replication copies DNA; transcription makes RNA from DNA.



Compare and contrast ribose with deoxyribose.

Ribose (RNA) has an extra oxygen atom compared to deoxyribose (DNA).


Be able to transcribe and translate a given DNA sequence (generate the resulting mRNA and amino acid sequences).  You will be given a copy of the genetic code table.

Transcribe: DNA to mRNA (A→U, T→A, C↔G). Translate: use codons to find amino acids.



Name the reasons why strawberries were chosen for DNA extraction.

Strawberries are octoploid (8 sets of chromosomes), soft, and yield lots of DNA.



Identify the material most commonly used to prepare the gel for electrophoresis.

Agarose.



Explain how gel electrophoresis separates molecules.

Molecules move through gel by size/charge; smaller move farther.



Identify the different beak adaptations evaluated and the food each was best suited for.

Different beak shapes suited for seeds, insects, nectar, etc.; adaptation matches food source.



Define protein clock theory and macroevolution.

Protein clock: molecular changes accumulate at steady rate. Macroevolution: large-scale evolutionary changes above species level.



Know the difference between a scientific name and a common name.

Scientific name: universal, Latin, two parts (genus, species). Common name: varies by region/language.
