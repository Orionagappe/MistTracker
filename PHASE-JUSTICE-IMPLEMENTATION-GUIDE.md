# PHASE JUSTICE: Implementation & Presentation Guide

**Codename:** JUSTICE  
**Document Type:** Internal Operational Guide  
**Date:** April 20, 2026  
**Purpose:** Guide for presenting and managing the settlement offer

---

## Executive Summary

This guide provides step-by-step instructions for:
1. Presenting the settlement offer to both parties
2. Managing acceptance/negotiation process
3. Verifying material release upon acceptance
4. Maintaining neutrality and compliance

**Critical Principle:** You remain completely uninvolved in decision-making. Your role is purely **facilitation and documentation**.

---

## Part 1: Pre-Presentation Preparation

### Step 1: Verify Your Access to Plaintiff Class

**Requirement:** Confirm you have legitimate contact with authorized plaintiff class representatives.

**Checklist:**
- ✓ Identify primary plaintiff class representative
- ✓ Verify they have authority to negotiate on behalf of class
- ✓ Confirm contact information (email, phone)
- ✓ Document that representative consents to you sharing offer
- ✓ Clarify if class has legal counsel (name/contact if yes)

**Action Item:**
Create a contact verification document including:
```
Plaintiff Class Contact Information:
- Primary Representative Name: _______________
- Title/Role: _______________
- Contact Email: _______________
- Contact Phone: _______________
- Authority to Negotiate: _______________
- Class Counsel (if any): _______________
- Counsel Contact: _______________
```

**Requirement:** Find authorized representative who can accept on behalf of developer.

**Research Needed:**

**Action Item:**

### Step 3: Determine Neutral Facilitator (Critical)

**Why This Matters:** You cannot present this offer yourself because you are "a party involved" (your words). A neutral third party MUST facilitate.

**Options for Neutral Facilitator:**

**Option A: Professional Mediator**
- American Arbitration Association (AAA)
- JAMS (Judicial Arbitration and Mediation Services)
- Local mediation services
- Cost: $1,500-$5,000+ depending on complexity

**Option B: Arbitrator (if already assigned)**
- If arbitration is already in progress, use the sitting arbitrator
- They are already neutral and have case knowledge
- Contact current arbitrator directly

**Option C: Law Professor/Expert Neutral**
- University-affiliated dispute resolution centers
- Often lower cost
- Ensure neutrality and legitimacy

**Your Action:**
1. Select neutral party option
2. Contact them to explain the case
3. Confirm they will facilitate
4. Get their contact information and credentials

**Template for Neutral Party Email:**

```
Subject: Mediation/Facilitation Request - [Game Project] Dispute

Dear [Neutral Party Name]:

I am writing to request your services as a neutral facilitator for a settlement 
negotiation in an ongoing dispute regarding [Game Project] between [Developer Name] 
and a class of customers.

Background:
- [1-2 sentence case summary]
- No money involved in this phase of settlement
- Offer involves release of documentation under Creative Commons
- Both parties have indicated openness to settlement discussion

Role Needed:
- Present settlement offer to both parties
- Manage communication between parties
- Document acceptance/rejection
- Oversee verification if accepted
- Maintain complete neutrality

Timeline: Decision needed by [specific date]

Would you be available and willing to serve in this capacity?

Fee arrangement: [Discuss with them]

Thank you,
[Your Name]
```

```
[Neutral Party Name] - The facilitator you selected
```

### Step 2: Create Simplified "Quick Summary" Version

Many people won't read the full legal document. Create one-page summary:

**PHASE JUSTICE: Settlement Offer (Quick Summary)**

```
WHAT IS BEING OFFERED?

The developer will release all documentation materials from the game 
project under a Creative Commons license, making them publicly available.

WHAT DOES THIS MEAN?

- Game design documents, plans, and notes become publicly accessible
- Anyone can view, study, and learn from the materials
- Materials will be under Creative Commons licensing
- No monetary payment involved in this offer

WHAT HAPPENS IF ACCEPTED?

- Settlement offer phase completes
- Both parties can negotiate final resolution
- Arbitration can move forward with better understanding of situation
- Documentation becomes permanent public record

TIMELINE?

- 7 days to review and respond
- 30 days to prepare materials
- 45 days to publicly release

DOES THIS MEAN THE DEVELOPER IS ADMITTING FAULT?

No. This is a settlement consideration that doesn't constitute admission 
of guilt or wrongdoing by anyone.

WHAT'S NEXT?

Both parties review, then respond to [Neutral Party] with acceptance or 
questions by [Date].
```

## Part 3: Presentation Process

### Step 1: Brief the Neutral Facilitator

Schedule call with neutral party (before contacting actual parties):

**Call Agenda (30 min):**
1. Explain case background in neutral terms
2. Explain the specific offer (CC material release)
3. Clarify your role (you step back after presentation)
4. Clarify their role (they facilitate all communication)
5. Set timeline and communication protocol
6. Discuss how they'll handle acceptance/rejection

**Key Point to Communicate:**
"After I give you these documents and the parties' contact info, I step back completely. 
You take over all communication and decision facilitation. I remain available for questions 
but do not influence the process."

---

### Step 2: Send Offer to Neutral Party First

Email neutral party:

**Subject:** PHASE JUSTICE Settlement Offer - Ready for Distribution

**Content:**
```
Dear [Neutral Party Name]:

Attached is the settlement offer for [case]. Please review for:
- Clarity
- Appropriateness
- Any concerns before presentation

Once you approve, I will provide you with both parties' contact information 
and you will distribute directly to them.

Key contacts:
- Plaintiff Class: [name/email/phone]
- Developer/Defendant: [name/email/phone]

I am available for questions but will not be involved in responses from 
either party. You will manage all communication from this point forward.

Suggested distribution date: [specific date/time]

Please confirm receipt and that you're ready to proceed.

Thank you,
[Your Name]
```

---

### Step 4: Your Step Back

**CRITICAL:** Once neutral party has information, you step completely back.

**You do NOT:**
- Contact either party directly about the offer
- Monitor their responses
- Participate in negotiations
- Suggest how they should respond
- Discuss offer details with either party

**Neutral Party does NOT:**
- Discuss offer with you before parties respond
- Tell you which party is leaning which direction
- Share party communications with you
- Consult you on their decision

**Exception - Only if asked:**
- If neutral party has procedural questions about the offer, you can clarify
- If parties ask about offer mechanics (not substance), neutral can redirect

## Part 4: Managing Responses

### Scenario 1: Both Parties Accept

**Neutral Party Action (Not Yours):**
1. Confirm acceptance from both parties
2. Send signed agreement to both parties
3. Establish material preparation timeline

**Your Role (Limited):**
- You may assist with logistics only:
  - Scheduling material review meetings
  - Creating file sharing systems
  - Documenting what materials were released
  - Verifying Creative Commons license was applied

**Critical:** Do NOT participate in deciding which materials to include/exclude or interpreting scope.

---

### Scenario 2: One Party Accepts, One Rejects

**Neutral Party Action:**
1. Documents acceptance/rejection
2. Notifies both parties of status
3. Informs parties that arbitration will resume (if that's the agreement)
4. May suggest next steps or further negotiation

**Your Role:**
- NONE. Process ends. Arbitration/litigation proceeds.

---

### Scenario 3: Counteroffer Received

**Example:** Developer wants to release materials under different license or with exclusions

**Neutral Party Action:**
1. Receives counteroffer
2. Presents to other party
3. Facilitates negotiation between parties
4. You are NOT involved

**Your Role:**
- NONE unless asked for clarification on original offer terms

---

### Scenario 4: Questions About the Offer

**If Plaintiff Class Asks:**
- Direct to neutral party
- Do not engage in answering beyond clarifying original document

**If Developer Asks:**
- Direct to neutral party
- Do not engage beyond clarifying original document

**Example Response:**
```
"Great question. Please direct all questions about the offer to [Neutral Party],
who is managing this process. They'll have the context to answer properly."
```

## Part 6: If Materials Are Released

### Verification Phase (IF Offer Accepted)

**Your Limited Role:**
Once materials are prepared and ready for release, you can verify:

1. **License Check**
   - Confirm Creative Commons license is properly applied
   - Verify license text is included with materials
   - Check that public repository displays license correctly

2. **Access Check**
   - Confirm materials are publicly accessible
   - Test that anyone can download/view
   - Verify license is readable

3. **Completeness Check**
   - Confirm materials match agreed scope
   - Verify excluded materials are properly excluded
   - Document what was released

**Verification Checklist:**
```
MATERIAL RELEASE VERIFICATION CHECKLIST

Materials Released: [Date]
Repository Location: [URL]
License Applied: [CC License Type]
Access Test: [Can public access? Yes/No]
License Visible: [Is license displayed? Yes/No]
Scope Compliance: [Matches agreement? Yes/No]
Excluded Items: [Properly excluded? Yes/No]

Verification Completed By: [Your Name]
Verification Date: [Date]
Status: VERIFIED / NEEDS CORRECTION

Notes/Issues: [Any problems found]
```

## Part 7: Escalation & Problems

### If Parties Dispute Scope

Example: "This material shouldn't be included" or "This material is missing"

**Your Role:**
- Document the dispute
- Forward to neutral party
- Do NOT make determination yourself
- Neutral party escalates to independent expert if needed

**Example Response:**
```
"I understand there's disagreement about scope. This kind of issue is exactly 
what the dispute resolution section of the offer addresses. Please document 
your concern and send it to [Neutral Party], who will facilitate resolution."
```

---

### If Developer Claims They Don't Own Materials

Example: "I can't release these because they contain licensed code"

**Your Role:**
- Do NOT dispute this claim
- Forward to neutral party
- Neutral party may require legal review
- Expert determination follows if needed

---

### If Plaintiff Class Rejects Released Materials

Example: "These materials are incomplete/misleading"

**Your Role:**
- Do NOT evaluate validity of claim
- Forward to neutral party
- Document the objection
- Neutral party determines if re-negotiation needed

## Part 9: Final Checklist Before Presenting Offer

**Pre-Presentation Verification:**

- ✓ Neutral party selected and confirmed
- ✓ Neutral party has reviewed offer for clarity
- ✓ Plaintiff class contact verified and authorized
- ✓ Developer contact verified and authorized
- ✓ Settlement offer document completed with all fields filled
- ✓ Quick summary version created
- ✓ Contact information provided to neutral party only
- ✓ Timeline specified (7-day response deadline)
- ✓ Communication protocol established
- ✓ Your role clearly understood (you step back)
- ✓ Neutral party role clearly understood (they facilitate)
- ✓ Escalation procedures documented
- ✓ Confidentiality guidelines confirmed

**Go/No-Go Decision:**
Before neutral party sends offer, confirm:
- Do all parties have legal representation? (If not, recommend it)
- Is neutral party truly neutral?
- Are you genuinely stepping back?
- Is timeline realistic?
- Are you prepared to accept either outcome (acceptance/rejection)?

## Part 10: Success Metrics

### Successful Offer Process

The offer process is successful if:

**Not about outcome acceptance/rejection, but about:**
1. ✓ Offer was presented fairly to both parties
2. ✓ Both parties understood the terms
3. ✓ Neutral party facilitated without bias
4. ✓ You remained completely uninvolved in decision-making
5. ✓ All communications were documented
6. ✓ Timeline was followed
7. ✓ Process maintained integrity and professionalism

### What DOESN'T Matter

- Whether parties accept or reject
- How parties respond
- Internal negotiations
- Which side "wins"
- Money involved or not

**Your only responsibility:** Facilitate fair process, then step back.
