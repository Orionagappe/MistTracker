## COMMON OBJECTIONS & RESPONSES

**Objection:** "Our current vendor isn't in the Challenge. Does that mean they're bad?"

**Response:** "Not necessarily. Challenge participation is optional. But if they won't compete, ask them why. If their detection rate is strong, they might not see the need. If it's weak, they might be avoiding the leaderboard. Request their data and compare to Challenge benchmarks independently."

---

**Objection:** "The Challenge only tested 5 vendors. That's not representative."

**Response:** "Fair point. 2027 will include 20+ teams. But even with 5, you have real comparative data. The industry average of 59.6% gives you a baseline. The vector breakdown tells you where industry strengths and weaknesses are. That's valuable intel whether the data comes from 5 or 50 teams."

---

**Objection:** "Threat detection is too custom for our environment. Generic benchmarks won't help."

**Response:** "Absolutely, your specific environment matters. But the Challenge vectors (SSH, SQL injection, timing attacks, etc.) are universal. The detection rates tell you what's possible. If a vendor claims 95% detection but only achieved 70% in the Challenge, that gap is meaningful for your environment too."

---

**Objection:** "We don't have time to implement Challenge recommendations."

**Response:** "Start small. Pick one weakness (e.g., timing attacks at 8% vs. 15% industry average) and address it this year. That single improvement might move your overall detection from 52% to 54%. Next year, tackle another. The Challenge gives you a roadmap—implement incrementally."
